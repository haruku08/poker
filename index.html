Hand rankings

From best (1) to worst (10). A hand always beats every hand below it. Only 5 cards count, even if you can choose from more.

  1. 1Royal flush

    A, K, Q, J, 10, all the same suit. The best possible hand.

    Tie? Split the pot. No suit is better than another.

  2. 2Straight flush

    Five cards in a row, all the same suit.

    Tie? The highest top card wins. 9-high beats 8-high.

  3. 3Four of a kind
    Also called "poker", "quads" or "carré"

    Four cards of the same rank, plus any fifth card.

    Tie? Higher four wins (four Kings beat four Queens). If the same, the fifth card decides.

  4. 4Full house
    Also called "full" or "boat"

    Three of a kind plus a pair.

    Tie? The three matching cards decide first: 8-8-8-4-4 beats 7-7-7-A-A. If the three are the same, the higher pair wins.

  5. 5Flush

    Any five cards of the same suit, not in a row.

    Tie? Compare the highest card, then the next, and so on. Suits don't matter.

  6. 6Straight
    Also called "street"

    Five cards in a row, mixed suits.

    Tie? The highest top card wins. An Ace can be high (10-J-Q-K-A, the best straight) or low (A-2-3-4-5, the lowest straight), but you can't go "around the corner" (Q-K-A-2-3 is not a straight).

  7. 7Three of a kind
    Also called "trips", "set" or "brelan"

    Three cards of the same rank.

    Tie? Higher three wins. If the same, the highest other card decides.

  8. 8Two pair

    Two different pairs.

    Tie? Compare the higher pair first, then the lower pair, then the fifth card.

  9. 9One pair

    Two cards of the same rank.

    Tie? Higher pair wins. If the same, compare the other three cards from the top down.

  10. 10High card

    Nothing of the above. Your highest card counts.

    Tie? Compare the highest card, then the next, and so on.

Texas Hold'em

The most popular poker game. 2 to 10 players, one normal 52-card deck, no jokers.

The idea in one sentence: everyone gets 2 secret cards, 5 shared cards are put face up in the middle, and you make the best 5-card hand from those 7 cards.

shared

Here your best hand is a pair of Kings with an Ace (K-K-A-9-7).

Before you start

Pick a dealer. A round "button" (any coin or token works) shows who the dealer is. After each hand, the button moves one seat to the left (clockwise), so everybody gets a turn.

The two players to the left of the dealer put in the blinds: a forced small bet so there is always something to play for. See Blinds & betting.

How a hand goes

  1. Blinds

    Player left of the dealer puts in the small blind. The next player puts in the big blind (usually double).

  2. Deal: 2 cards each

    Everyone gets 2 cards face down, one at a time, starting left of the dealer. Look at them, don't show anyone.

    Betting round 1 ("pre-flop"). Starts with the player left of the big blind. You must at least match the big blind to stay in.

  3. The flop: 3 cards

    The dealer puts 3 shared cards face up in the middle.

    Betting round 2. From now on, betting starts with the first player still in, left of the dealer.

  4. The turn: 1 card

    A 4th shared card is put face up.

    Betting round 3.

  5. The river: 1 card

    The 5th and last shared card is put face up.

    Betting round 4. The last chance to bet.

  6. Showdown

    If more than one player is still in, they show their cards. The best 5-card hand wins the whole pot. Equal hands split the pot.

Winning without showing If everyone else folds (gives up), the last player left wins the pot right away and doesn't have to show their cards.

Using your cards

  • You can use both, one or none of your own 2 cards.
  • If the best hand is just the 5 shared cards ("playing the board"), everyone still in ties and splits the pot, unless someone can do better with their own cards.
  • Only the best 5 cards count. A 6th or 7th card never helps break a tie.
Burn cards (optional) In casinos the dealer throws away the top card face down before the flop, the turn and the river. At home you can skip this, but it helps against accidentally seeing the next card.

Blinds & betting

How much do I have to put in, and when?

The pot

The pot is all the chips in the middle. Whoever wins the hand takes the whole pot.

Small blind and big blind

Before the cards are dealt, two players must put in chips:

  • Small blind (SB): the player directly left of the dealer. Puts in a small amount, for example 1 chip.
  • Big blind (BB): the next player to the left. Puts in double, for example 2 chips.
  • The player left of the big blind is the first to act before the flop.
  • The blinds move with the dealer button, so everyone pays them in turn.
Only 2 players? The dealer is the small blind and acts first before the flop. The other player is the big blind. After the flop, the big blind acts first.

What you can do on your turn

CheckPass without betting. Only if nobody has bet yet this round.
BetBe the first to put chips in this round.
CallMatch the current bet to stay in.
RaiseMatch the bet and add more.
FoldGive up. Your cards go away and you lose what you already put in.
All-inPut in all your chips.

When does a betting round end?

When everyone still in has put in the same amount this round (or everyone checked). Then the next cards are dealt. A player who has already acted gets another turn if someone raises after them.

How much can I bet?

good for kids		Bets are a fixed size: 1 big blind in rounds 1 and 2, 2 big blinds in rounds 3 and 4. Max 1 bet + 3 raises per round.

Raise rules

  • The smallest bet is one big blind.
  • A raise must be at least as big as the previous bet or raise. Example: someone bets 4, you can raise to 8 or more (not to 6).
  • Say "raise" before you put chips in. Putting in chips one by one ("string bet") doesn't count as a raise, only as a call.

Example round

Blinds are 1 and 2. Anna is small blind (puts in 1), Ben is big blind (puts in 2).

Chris is first: calls 2. Dana raises to 6. Anna folds (her 1 stays in the pot). Ben calls 4 more (6 total). Chris calls 4 more.

Everyone left has put in 6, so the round is over. Pot: 1 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 19. Now the flop.

All-in and side pots

You can never be forced out because you don't have enough chips. Put in everything you have ("all-in") and you stay in until the showdown, but you can only win from each other player as much as you put in.

Extra bets from the others go into a separate side pot that only they can win.

Example: Sam goes all-in with 10. Lisa and Tom both bet 30. The main pot is 30 (10 from each) and anyone can win it. The side pot is 40 (the extra 20 from Lisa and Tom), and only Lisa or Tom can win that.

Quick answers

The questions that always start a discussion at the table.

Is a full house higher than a flush?

Yes. Full house beats flush.

beats

Is a flush higher than four of a kind ("poker")?

No. Four of a kind beats a flush, and also beats a full house.

beats

Only a straight flush or royal flush beats four of a kind.

What exactly is a straight flush?

Five cards in a row AND all the same suit.

Same suit but not in a row is just a flush. In a row but mixed suits is just a straight. You need both. It beats everything except a higher straight flush (and the royal flush is simply the highest straight flush).

Does a flush beat a straight?

Yes. Flush beats straight.

Easy to remember: a flush is harder to get, so it's worth more.

Does a straight beat three of a kind?

Yes. Straight beats three of a kind.

Is A-2-3-4-5 a straight?

Yes, it's the lowest straight (called the "wheel").

It counts as 5-high, so 2-3-4-5-6 beats it.

Is Q-K-A-2-3 a straight?

No. You can't go around the corner.

The Ace is either at the top (10-J-Q-K-A) or at the bottom (A-2-3-4-5), never in the middle.

Is one suit better than another?

No. All suits are equal.

A hearts flush and a spades flush with the same cards tie and split the pot.

Two players have the same pair. Who wins?

The player with the highest other card (the "kicker").

beats

Both have a pair of Kings, but the Ace beats the Queen.

Two full houses. Who wins?

The higher three-of-a-kind part wins.

beats

Three 9s beat three 8s. The pair only matters if the three are equal.

I have 3 pairs. Is that worth more?

No. Only 5 cards count, so it's just two pair.

Use your two highest pairs, and the best remaining card as the fifth.

Can there be a tie?

Yes. If the best 5 cards are equal, the pot is split evenly.

An odd chip that can't be split goes to the first winner left of the dealer.

Do I have to show my cards if I win?

Only at the showdown.

If everyone else folded, you win without showing. At a showdown, the last player who bet or raised shows first. If nobody bet in the last round, the first player left of the dealer shows first.

Someone has fewer chips than the bet. Are they out?

No. They can go all-in with what they have.

See "All-in and side pots" under Blinds & betting.

Is there a "five of a kind"?

Not in normal poker.

A 52-card deck only has four of each rank. Five of a kind is only possible if you play with jokers or wild cards.

Other games

Same hand rankings, different way of dealing. Blinds and betting work like Texas Hold'em unless it says otherwise.

Omaha

Like Texas Hold'em, but everyone gets 4 cards instead of 2.

yours
shared

The big rule: you must use exactly 2 of your own cards and exactly 3 shared cards. Not more, not less.

Above, there are 4 hearts on the table and you have the A♥. In Texas Hold'em that would be a flush. In Omaha it is not: you need 2 hearts from your own hand. Your best hand here is just Ace high (A-K + Q-J-7).

Usually played pot-limit ("PLO"). Hands are much stronger than in Hold'em, so a pair rarely wins.

Five Card Draw

The classic "kitchen table" poker. No shared cards. Good for beginners and kids.

  1. Everyone puts in a small ante (for example 1 chip), or use blinds like Hold'em.
  2. Everyone gets 5 cards face down.
  3. Betting round 1, starting left of the dealer.
  4. The draw: in turn, each player may throw away 0 to 3 cards and gets the same number of new ones. (Many home games allow 4 if your last card is an Ace.)
  5. Betting round 2.
  6. Showdown: best 5-card hand wins.

Seven Card Stud

No blinds and no shared cards. Some of your cards are face up so everyone can see them. Best for 2 to 7 players.

  1. Everyone puts in an ante.
  2. Deal 2 cards face down and 1 face up to each player.
    The player with the lowest face-up card must start with a small forced bet (the "bring-in"). Betting round 1.
  3. Deal a 4th card face up. Betting round 2.
  4. Deal a 5th card face up. Betting round 3.
  5. Deal a 6th card face up. Betting round 4.
  6. Deal a 7th card face down. Betting round 5.
  7. Showdown: best 5 of your 7 cards wins.

From the 4th card on, the player with the best face-up cards starts each betting round.

Fun home rules

Wild cards: agree before the game that, for example, all 2s ("deuces wild") or jokers count as any card you want. With wild cards, five of a kind becomes possible and is the best hand of all, above the royal flush.

Always agree on house rules before the first hand.
